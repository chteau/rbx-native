Joints are now soft (a 60 Hz spring: the arm stretches like Roblox's, 4.01 against 4.046 at the bottom), but the period still differs a little and the difference grows with time; no single spring stiffness fits both this swing and HingeFree.
- **HumanoidJump**: JumpPower jumps match; a JumpHeight jump of 15 studs (UseJumpPower false) launches at 74.27 against 75.09 and peaks 0.2 stud low. Roblox's height-to-speed conversion has a small extra term the fit doesn't have yet.
- **HumanoidSlope**: 30 degree slopes match. On 60 degrees ours walks up and off the top at the slope's speed, where Roblox stutters at the top edge and launches higher (12.8 against 8.6 rise at 90 frames); with MaxSlopeAngle 45 Roblox slides back down a too-steep slope (v -3.55) and ours creeps up it. Simply refusing to walk up a too-steep floor sends ours sliding off the wedge's far side (tried in M10); the wall-and-edge behaviour of the floor sensor on steep wedges isn't modelled.
- **HumanoidPlatform**: A rider on an unanchored raft carried by LinearVelocity matches, and so does the horizontal carry on an anchored lift moved by CFrame every beat; but on that lift ours ends 0.9 stud lower than Roblox's passenger (-3.17 against -2.27): how Roblox's floor sensor keeps a character on a part teleported by CFrame isn't pinned down.
- **ContactSink**: Single blocks of any density and stacks of equal blocks sink like Roblox's within 0.01 stud; a dense block on a light one sinks the light one 0.015 stud in its first second where Roblox's sinks 0.031 (and 0.098 against 0.082 after two). Roblox's contact stiffness depends on the mass ratio in a way one spring doesn't cover.
